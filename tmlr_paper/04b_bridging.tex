\section{Bridging Representational and Reasoning Superposition}
\label{sec:bridging}

Recent work on chain-of-continuous-thought (Coconut) \citep{hao2024training} has introduced a distinct notion of superposition in the context of reasoning. While \citet{elhage2022toy} study \emph{representational superposition}---packing more features than dimensions---\citet{zhu2025reasoning} analyze \emph{reasoning superposition}---maintaining multiple reasoning traces simultaneously in latent space. We prove that these phenomena are mathematically equivalent, arising from the same optimization principle. Critically, this is not merely a theoretical claim: Figure~\ref{fig:interference_comparison} shows that a representational model (\texttt{continuous\_thought}) and a reasoning model (\texttt{coconut}) trained on different objectives converge to geometrically indistinguishable encodings at the same bottleneck dimension $d=256$, providing direct empirical evidence for the unification.

\subsection{Preliminaries}

\begin{definition}[Superposition Encoding]
Let $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, \ldots, s_n\}$ be a set of $n$ objects and let $\mathbf{W} = [\mathbf{w}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{w}_n] \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ be an encoding matrix with $m < n$. We say $\mathbf{W}$ is a \emph{superposition encoding} if there exist $i \neq j$ such that $\langle \mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{w}_j \rangle \neq 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Co-occurrence Matrix]
A \emph{co-occurrence matrix} $P \in [0,1]^{n \times n}$ specifies the probability that objects $s_i$ and $s_j$ are simultaneously relevant, with $P_{ii} = p_i$ denoting the marginal probability that object $s_i$ is relevant.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The Equivalence Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Representational-Reasoning Superposition Equivalence]
\label{thm:equivalence}
Let $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^{m \times n} \times [0,1]^{n \times n} \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}, P) = \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i \left(1 - \|\mathbf{w}_i\|^2\right)^2}_{\text{capacity loss}} + \underbrace{\sum_{i \neq j} P_{ij} \langle \mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{w}_j \rangle^2}_{\text{interference loss}}
\label{eq:master_loss}
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{(Representational Superposition)} The expected reconstruction loss of \citet{elhage2022toy} equals $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}, P)$ when $P_{ij} = p_i p_j$.
    \item[(b)] \textbf{(Reasoning Superposition)} The implicit objective minimized by the attention mechanism in \citet{zhu2025reasoning} equals $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}, P)$ when $P_{ij} = \mathbf{1}[\text{traces } i,j \text{ both valid}]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\textbf{Part (a):} Expanding the expected reconstruction error of the autoencoder with encoder $\mathbf{W}$ and tied decoder $\mathbf{W}^\top$, each component's error decomposes into a capacity term $(1 - \|\mathbf{w}_i\|^2)^2$ and interference terms $\langle \mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{w}_j \rangle^2$ weighted by the probability of co-activation $p_i p_j$, yielding $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}, P)$ with $P_{ij} = p_i p_j$.

\textbf{Part (b):} In the continuous chain-of-thought setting of \citet{zhu2025reasoning}, the thought vector $\mathbf{h}_t = \sum_k \alpha_k^{(t)} \mathbf{v}_k$ superposes $K$ reasoning traces. The attention mechanism implicitly minimizes a loss preserving valid traces ($\|\mathbf{v}_k\| \approx 1$) while reducing interference ($\langle \mathbf{v}_i, \mathbf{v}_j \rangle \approx 0$). Setting $P_{ij} = \mathbf{1}[i \in V \land j \in V]$ recovers $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}, P)$. Full proofs are given in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sparsity-Capacity Bound]
\label{cor:capacity}
Let $S = 1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,j} P_{ij}$ be the sparsity of co-occurrence. For any encoding $\mathbf{W}$ achieving interference loss at most $\epsilon$, the number of objects with $\|\mathbf{w}_i\| \geq 1/2$ is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
|\{i : \|\mathbf{w}_i\| \geq 1/2\}| \leq m + \frac{m(m-1)}{2} \cdot \frac{S}{\epsilon}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At most $m$ vectors can be mutually orthogonal in $\mathbb{R}^m$. A packing argument on the Gram matrix $\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{W}^\top \mathbf{W}$, combined with the constraint $\sum_{i \neq j} P_{ij} G_{ij}^2 \leq \epsilon$, yields the bound. See Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

This corollary formalizes why both paradigms benefit from sparsity: in representational superposition, sparse feature activations reduce $P_{ij}$; in reasoning superposition, the branching structure of valid traces creates sparse co-validity patterns. Empirically, this sparsity dependence is confirmed in Figure~\ref{fig:interference_sparsity}: at $p=1.0$ (no sparsity, maximum $P_{ij}$) the interference matrix is dense and chaotic, while at $p=0.05$ (high sparsity, small $P_{ij}$) the model finds near-structured encodings. The phase diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:geometric_analysis}) maps the full transition: yellow regions (low $p$, high importance) correspond to superposition regimes where sparsity enables features to share dimensions; blue regions (high $p$) correspond to the dense regime where features must be dropped or collapsed.

The feature dimensionality curves (Figure~\ref{fig:feature_dim_curves}) further validate the corollary's quantitative prediction. Features undergo discontinuous transitions at a critical $p^*$ rather than smoothly degrading---exactly the behavior predicted when the interference cost $P_{ij} G_{ij}^2$ crosses the capacity benefit threshold.

\subsection{Time-Space Equivalence}

\begin{theorem}[Iterative Refinement Increases Effective Capacity]
\label{thm:timespace}
Let $\mathcal{M}_1(d)$ be a single-step model with hidden dimension $d$, and let $\mathcal{M}_T(d)$ be a model with the same dimension but $T$ iterative refinement steps, where each step applies a transformation $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ with a gating mechanism. If $f_\theta$ can compute:
\begin{equation}
f_\theta\left(\sum_k \alpha_k \mathbf{v}_k\right) = \sum_k \alpha_k \cdot g_k(\{\alpha_j, \mathbf{v}_j\}_{j=1}^K) \cdot \mathbf{v}_k
\end{equation}
for learnable modulation functions $g_k: \mathbb{R}^{K(d+1)} \to \mathbb{R}$, then $\mathcal{M}_T(d)$ can represent any function computable by $\mathcal{M}_1(d \cdot 2^{T-1})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By induction on $T$. Each refinement step can use gating to partition superposed traces into two groups, doubling the effective capacity. After $T$ steps, this yields $d \cdot 2^{T-1}$ effective dimensions. See Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

This theorem establishes that iterative reasoning (temporal depth) provides an exponential increase in effective representational capacity, explaining why continuous chain-of-thought can solve problems requiring superposition of many traces despite limited hidden dimensions.

The emergence of equiangular structure during training (Figure~\ref{fig:weight_progression}) supports the mechanism: as training progresses, feature vectors spread from random initialization into approximately uniform angular configurations, consistent with the equiangular tight frame construction that achieves the Welch lower bound. Both the representational and reasoning models converge to this structure, indicating both are solving the same vector-packing problem. Furthermore, the polysemanticity analysis (Section~\ref{sec:results}) shows that 77\% of neurons in the $d=256$ bottleneck model are polysemantic, directly demonstrating that superposition is not a pathology of simple settings but persists in practical language models operating in the temporal-refinement regime.

\subsection{Implications}

The connection between representational and reasoning superposition has several implications:

\paragraph{For interpretability.} Techniques developed for analyzing representational superposition (e.g., sparse autoencoders) may transfer to reasoning superposition. Conversely, the ``superposition state'' interpretation from \citet{zhu2025reasoning} suggests new ways to visualize feature interference.

\paragraph{For architecture design.} The tradeoff between spatial capacity (width) and temporal capacity (depth/iterations) suggests that narrow-but-deep architectures with iterative refinement may achieve similar expressiveness to wider architectures, while being more amenable to interpretation.

\paragraph{For AI safety.} If reasoning in language models involves superposition of multiple traces, then ``steering'' model behavior \citep{templeton2024scaling} may require understanding which traces are superposed and how to selectively suppress harmful ones without disrupting beneficial reasoning.
