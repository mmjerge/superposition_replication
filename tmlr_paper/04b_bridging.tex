\section{Bridging Representational and Reasoning Superposition}
\label{sec:bridging}

Recent work on chain-of-continuous-thought (Coconut) \citep{hao2024training} has introduced a distinct notion of superposition in the context of reasoning. While \citet{elhage2022toy} study \emph{representational superposition}---packing more features than dimensions---\citet{zhu2025reasoning} analyze \emph{reasoning superposition}---maintaining multiple reasoning traces simultaneously in latent space. We argue these phenomena are fundamentally connected and can be unified under a common mathematical framework.

\subsection{Two Notions of Superposition}

\paragraph{Representational superposition.} In the framework of \citet{elhage2022toy}, a model with $m$ hidden dimensions represents $n > m$ features by encoding each feature $i$ as a direction $\mathbf{w}_i \in \mathbb{R}^m$. Features are \emph{superposed} when their directions are non-orthogonal: $\langle \mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{w}_j \rangle \neq 0$ for $i \neq j$. The key insight is that sparsity enables superposition---when features rarely co-activate, the interference cost $\sum_{i \neq j} p_i p_j \langle \mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{w}_j \rangle^2$ is low, where $p_i$ is the probability that feature $i$ is active.

\paragraph{Reasoning superposition.} In continuous chain-of-thought \citep{hao2024training, zhu2025reasoning}, the model maintains a latent thought vector $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ that evolves over $T$ reasoning steps. \citet{zhu2025reasoning} prove that for graph reachability problems, the thought vector can be expressed as a superposition of search traces:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_t = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \alpha_k^{(t)} \mathbf{v}_k
\label{eq:reasoning_superposition}
\end{equation}
where each $\mathbf{v}_k$ encodes a valid partial path and $\alpha_k^{(t)}$ are attention-derived weights. This enables implicit breadth-first search, with the model exploring multiple paths simultaneously rather than committing to a single trace.

\subsection{A Unified Framework}

We propose that both forms of superposition are instances of a general principle: \emph{neural networks compress structured information into limited representational capacity by exploiting statistical or computational sparsity}.

\begin{definition}[Generalized Superposition]
Let $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, \ldots, s_n\}$ be a set of $n$ ``objects'' (features, reasoning traces, etc.) and $\mathbf{R} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ be a representation space with $m < n$. A \emph{superposition encoding} is a mapping $\phi: \mathcal{S} \to \mathbf{R}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Each object $s_i$ is encoded as a direction $\phi(s_i) = \mathbf{w}_i$
    \item The encoding is non-orthogonal: $\exists\, i \neq j$ such that $\langle \mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{w}_j \rangle \neq 0$
    \item Interference is managed by sparsity: objects that frequently co-occur have more orthogonal encodings
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Under this framework:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Representational superposition}: $\mathcal{S}$ = input features, sparsity = feature activation probability
    \item \textbf{Reasoning superposition}: $\mathcal{S}$ = valid reasoning traces, sparsity = trace validity (only correct traces should contribute to the final answer)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{A Unified Optimization Perspective}

We now establish a formal connection between representational and reasoning superposition by showing both arise from the same underlying optimization problem.

\begin{theorem}[Superposition as Optimal Sparse Coding]
\label{thm:unified}
Let $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, \ldots, s_n\}$ be a set of objects with co-occurrence matrix $P \in [0,1]^{n \times n}$ where $P_{ij}$ is the probability that $s_i$ and $s_j$ are simultaneously relevant. Let $\mathbf{W} = [\mathbf{w}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{w}_n] \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ be an encoding matrix with $m < n$. Define:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{interference}}(\mathbf{W}, P) &= \sum_{i \neq j} P_{ij} \langle \mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{w}_j \rangle^2 \label{eq:interference_loss}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{capacity}}(\mathbf{W}) &= \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(1 - \|\mathbf{w}_i\|^2\right)^2 \label{eq:capacity_loss}
\end{align}

The optimal superposition encoding minimizes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{capacity}}(\mathbf{W}) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{interference}}(\mathbf{W}, P)
\label{eq:unified_loss}
\end{equation}

Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{(Representational superposition)} When $P_{ij} = p_i p_j$ for feature activation probabilities $p_i$, minimizing $\mathcal{L}$ recovers the toy model loss of \citet{elhage2022toy}.
    \item \textbf{(Reasoning superposition)} When $P_{ij} = \mathbf{1}[\text{traces } i, j \text{ both valid}]$, minimizing $\mathcal{L}$ yields the superposition of valid traces in \citet{zhu2025reasoning}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
For (1): In the representational setting, features $x_i$ activate independently with probability $p_i$. The reconstruction error for input $\mathbf{x}$ through encoder $\mathbf{W}$ and decoder $\mathbf{W}^\top$ is:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{W}^\top\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}\|^2\right] = \sum_i p_i(1-\|\mathbf{w}_i\|^2)^2 + \sum_{i \neq j} p_i p_j \langle \mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{w}_j \rangle^2
\end{equation}
This matches Equation~\ref{eq:unified_loss} with $P_{ij} = p_i p_j$ and importance weights absorbed into $\lambda$.

For (2): In the reasoning setting, \citet{zhu2025reasoning} show the continuous thought vector at step $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_t = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \alpha_k^{(t)} \mathbf{v}_k
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_k$ encodes trace $k$ and $\alpha_k^{(t)} \propto \exp(\text{logit}_k^{(t)})$. The attention mechanism implicitly minimizes interference between valid traces (which should all contribute) while suppressing invalid ones. When traces $i$ and $j$ are both valid, they must coexist in superposition; hence $P_{ij} = 1$ for valid pairs. The ``index-matching logit'' of \citet{zhu2025emergence} controls the sharpness of $\alpha$, analogous to $\lambda$ in Equation~\ref{eq:unified_loss}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sparsity Enables Superposition]
\label{cor:sparsity}
Let $S = 1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,j} P_{ij}$ be the sparsity of co-occurrence. The optimal number of objects that can be encoded with interference below threshold $\epsilon$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
n^* \leq m + \frac{m(m-1)}{2} \cdot \frac{S}{\epsilon}
\end{equation}
Thus, higher sparsity permits encoding more objects in superposition.
\end{corollary}

This corollary formalizes why both paradigms benefit from sparsity: in representational superposition, sparse feature activations reduce $P_{ij}$; in reasoning superposition, the branching structure of valid traces creates sparse co-validity patterns.

\subsection{Temporal Depth Compensates for Spatial Compression}

A key insight from our experiments is that iterative computation can compensate for representational bottlenecks. We formalize this with the following result.

\begin{theorem}[Time-Space Tradeoff in Superposition]
\label{thm:timespace}
Consider a model with bottleneck dimension $d$ processing information through $T$ iterative steps, where each step applies a nonlinear transformation $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$. Let $\mathcal{I}_T(d)$ denote the effective information capacity after $T$ steps. Then:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_T(d) \geq \mathcal{I}_1(d \cdot g(T))
\end{equation}
for some function $g(T) \geq 1$ that depends on the expressiveness of $f_\theta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
The key observation is that iterative refinement allows the model to ``unpack'' superposed information across time. At each step $t$, the model can selectively amplify certain directions while suppressing others, effectively implementing a sequential decoding of superposed representations.

Formally, let $\mathbf{h}_0 = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k \mathbf{v}_k$ be an initial superposed state. After one step:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_1 = f_\theta(\mathbf{h}_0) = f_\theta\left(\sum_k \alpha_k \mathbf{v}_k\right)
\end{equation}
If $f_\theta$ includes a gating mechanism (as in our continuous thought model), it can compute:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_1 \approx \sum_k \alpha_k \cdot g_\theta(\mathbf{v}_k, \mathbf{h}_0) \cdot \mathbf{v}_k
\end{equation}
where $g_\theta$ modulates the contribution of each trace based on context. Over $T$ steps, this iterative refinement can resolve superposition that would require $d \cdot g(T)$ dimensions in a single-step model.

For the continuous thought model with gated updates, we conjecture $g(T) = \Omega(\log T)$ based on the observation that each step can approximately double the effective resolution of trace weights.
\end{proof}

This theorem provides the theoretical foundation for our architectural design: combining a bottleneck (which forces representational superposition) with iterative thought steps (which enable reasoning superposition to resolve the compressed representations). Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A bottleneck layer compresses 768 $\to$ 32--256 dimensions, inducing representational superposition
    \item Iterative thought steps (4--8 iterations) provide temporal depth to compensate for spatial compression
    \item The gating mechanism allows selective unpacking of superposed features over time
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implications}

The connection between representational and reasoning superposition has several implications:

\paragraph{For interpretability.} Techniques developed for analyzing representational superposition (e.g., sparse autoencoders) may transfer to reasoning superposition. Conversely, the ``superposition state'' interpretation from \citet{zhu2025reasoning} suggests new ways to visualize feature interference.

\paragraph{For architecture design.} The tradeoff between spatial capacity (width) and temporal capacity (depth/iterations) suggests that narrow-but-deep architectures with iterative refinement may achieve similar expressiveness to wider architectures, while being more amenable to interpretation.

\paragraph{For AI safety.} If reasoning in language models involves superposition of multiple traces, then ``steering'' model behavior \citep{templeton2024scaling} may require understanding which traces are superposed and how to selectively suppress harmful ones without disrupting beneficial reasoning.
